Every note the lesson plays (splendid-piano.js) is the Splendid Grand Piano: Steinway samples that Akai released into the public domain in 2000, mapped to SFZ by kinwie (https://github.com/sfzinstruments/SplendidGrandPiano), in the Ogg Opus encodes of https://github.com/smpldsnds/sfzinstruments-splendid-grand-piano, served unchanged from splendid/ (with an AAC copy where a browser cannot play Ogg Opus). Which sample sounds each key at each loudness follows smplr 1.1.0 by danigb (https://github.com/danigb/smplr), under the MIT licence (https://opensource.org/licenses/MIT); no smplr code is served.

Where the lesson's player bar shows a cover for a composer, it is a painting in the public domain: Joseph Karl Stieler, Beethoven with the manuscript of the Missa solemnis (1820), from Wikimedia Commons (https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Beethoven.jpg), scaled down.

Where a test's agreement names it, Muse's lines in the lesson are spoken by ElevenLabs (https://elevenlabs.io), a text-to-speech service, in a voice from its voice library: the words of the lesson are sent to it and nothing else (no video, no sound, no picture).
