Under the hood

How it works

The teacher watches a video of you at the piano and listens to it. Our own code measures what happened and decides, with fixed limits, what is worth saying. Meta's models find the keyboard and your hands in the picture, put the decided remarks into words, write a practice plan from them and answer your questions from the measurements.

From your video to the answers

Unless this server is set otherwise, 4 frames spread over the part analysed go through SAM 3.1 before the rest. When they show no keyboard, or no end of it with your hands at the edge of the picture, the full run would very likely not place your hands either, so the take is judged from its notes at once (its sound, or your piano's MIDI), and the lesson says why and how to film the next one. It can be wrong: filmed at a slant, or with a second keyboard in the picture, an end can be missed in those 4 frames. A longer video is listened to whole (its first hour at most), and only the part with the most piano in it is analysed: a minute, unless this server is set otherwise.

Which model does what, and where it runs here

Each line says whether this server uses that model now, read from its own settings.

  • SAM 3.1

    Meta

    Finds the keyboard, every black key, your hands, thumbs and forearms in the frames, from text prompts, at 5 frames a second. Our own solver turns those outlines into where every key is and where your hands are on them: SAM 3.1 says what is where, never the final geometry. Where it runs gets the frames, never the sound.

  • Muse Spark

    Meta

    The teacher you talk with: says the lesson in its own voice (every line checked against what was measured: the same notes in the same order, the same bars, hands and fingers, else the lesson's own words stand), puts the remarks the measurements decided into words, answers your questions through read-only lookups of the measured take, and writes the practice plan when Muse Wonder cannot. An upload reaches it only when its player agreed to that at upload and confirmed they are 18 or older; the demos, recorded in the Museverse Quest app, are not sent to it unless Meta has agreed to that in writing. Its words are said aloud by your browser's own voice, or, where this server uses it and the player agreed, by ElevenLabs, a voice service that receives only the words; they are always on screen too.

  • Muse Wonder

    Meta

    The deeper reasoning about your performance. It judges the lesson: of the parts the measurements found (a slip that came back every time, one you put right, a finding), which to teach, in what order and why, with a drill for each and a short verdict on the take. It is given the teacher's know-how (how the take was measured, how sure each kind of evidence is, how a teacher chooses) and the evidence, never the video; it can leave out a slip you put right yourself but never a habit, and cannot add a part or a note. Every line it writes is checked against the evidence, and a choice that fails keeps the lesson's own order. It also writes the practice plan: about ten minutes in three to five blocks, each an exercise from the list of a finding the measurements decided (or, once at most, a check to keep doing something you did well), with one reason in that finding's own words.

  • Muse Spark Open

    Meta's open-weights model

    Does Muse Spark's jobs, the plan included, at an open-weights host: an inference provider or our own server. Only for uploads whose player agreed to that host by name; never for a demo.

  • Muse Glimmer

    Meta

    Self-hosted on our own server, so nothing goes to Meta: it words the remarks and answers questions, without lookups, every reply checked by the same rules as the spoken lines, and it does not write the plan. Through OpenRouter or at an open-weights host, only for uploads whose player agreed to that host, it does Muse Spark's jobs instead: the lookups, the evidence check and the plan, and at an open-weights host the filming tip too.

  • Muse Voice Transcribe

    Meta

    Turns a question you speak into words, after you agree that your voice goes to Meta and confirm you are 18 or older. Typing always works.

  • The filming tip

    Muse Spark, Muse Spark Open or Muse Glimmer

    Only when you tick its own box at upload, and only for a badly framed video: the hosted route's model (Muse Spark, or the open-weights host's Muse Spark Open or Muse Glimmer; never Muse Glimmer through OpenRouter) looks at the 4 frames of the framing check and picks one tip from our list. It never changes the verdict; you read our words for the tip.

  • Piano transcription

    an open model, not Meta (Kong et al. 2020)

    Hears the notes in the sound: when each one starts, and roughly how loud it is (the loudness it hears follows the piano's with a correlation of 0.70, below), so loudness is not judged from the sound. When you add the MIDI your digital piano recorded while you filmed, and it lines up with this take, the lesson uses those exact notes instead.

  • The teacher's voice

    your browser

    Meta has no text-to-speech yet, so your browser reads the teacher's lines aloud with its own voice.

What decides, and what a model may do

Measurements decide every finding

  • Each item is measured phrase by phrase and judged against your own other phrases and, where it has one, a fixed limit: a chord spread over more than 50 ms is heard as a roll, and left and right notes written together that land more than 60 ms apart are hands apart. Under 100 ms that is asked as a question (did you mean the bass to come first?), since a bass played first on purpose is often about 50 ms early; only from 100 ms is it worded as a correction.
  • An item is judged only from an input that can measure it (the table below). What a take's input cannot judge is left out, and the lesson says so and why, as in "Loudness is only roughly heard from a video's sound, so it is not judged."
  • A technique remark needs at least 2 flagged phrases. An item counts as judged only when it was measured in 2 phrases where it has a fixed limit, and in 4 where it is compared with your own playing alone; a take with nothing judged is never told that nothing stood out: it is told it was too short, or, when it had 30 seconds and 4 phrases of playing or more, that nothing it can judge came up often enough in it (from the sound, a quiet minute with few chords heard clearly is like that).
  • Wrong and missed notes against a score are said only above what the transcription gets wrong on its own: half of the score's notes in the part you played missed (8 at least), or a fifth of them wrong (4 at least). On your piano's own MIDI, two notes played differently from the score in one bar are enough.
  • Before any of that, the score alignment's own slips are put right or set aside. It matches by pitch alone, so on a key the music repeats it can take each note for the one before; those are paired again in order, each only with a note played where the notes around it place it. A written note and a played note of the same key within half a second of where it was expected are not counted as a missed and an added note either: that may be the alignment's slip or two neighbouring notes swapped, so it is never blamed and never earns praise. A note left out, or played a beat or more away from its place, is still found. From the sound, the notes are listed one by one only when a finding was raised, since the sound alone mishears notes. A key heard different counts from the sound even once when it is clear: as loud as the notes around it, held 0.15 s or more, played alone (in a chord, the voices the transcriber adds and misses pair up as wrong notes), and not held while the written key starts. Two clear keys are a finding on their own, named bar by bar. With no score given, the library piece the notes play is the score. Stops and going back are found too: a stop is a pause of half a second or more, at least twice the time the notes there should take, inside an even run of notes with the playing at its own pace just before and after it, so a gradual slowing or a free rhythm is never one; going back is five or more notes of the melody played again. On a professional's takes neither is found.
  • A bar's timing is judged only from notes on the beat of the notes around them: a bar where a note lands more than 120 ms off that, or holds a note set aside as a possible slip, is not timed at all, since that note may be the alignment's slip as easily as yours.
  • No model decides, adds or removes a finding.

What a model may do

  • Word the remarks already decided. A line with any number other than the times it was given, a word on our banned list or the wrong hand is replaced by our fixed line.
  • Answer through 8 read-only lookups of the measured take (overview, a finding, the notes in a bar, the notes between two times, a phrase, exercises, a moment, and a re-take's comparison), at most 3 rounds and, unless this server sets another limit, 20 seconds for one answer.
  • Choose a plan's exercises from the decided findings' own lists. Every id, exercise, minute and reason is checked before the plan is used.
  • Pick one of our 10 filming tips, or none. A tip the framing check does not allow for its cause, or none, gives our own fixed tip for that cause.

The evidence check

  • In an answer written from lookups, every time, bar, note, count and measured number must be one the lookups returned or the take's own context, given to the model, holds.
  • An answer that says something got better or worse since the first take must say what the comparison of the two takes says.
  • Anything else, and the answer is replaced by our fixed answer. The page shows what the model looked up.

Fallbacks, never a guess

  • No model set up, a reply that fails a check, an error or a timeout: our fixed wording or fixed answer.
  • The plan: Muse Wonder, then Muse Spark (where the model that answers is Muse Spark Open or a hosted Muse Glimmer, that model alone), then our fixed plan from the top findings' own exercises, within 40 seconds for all of it unless this server sets another limit. A demo's plan is always the fixed one.
  • The filming tip: our fixed tip after 15 seconds or on any error.
  • A question about pain always gets our fixed answer (stop, rest, and talk to a piano teacher or a doctor), and so does a question about something the take cannot judge.
  • Never a contributor model, one that trains on what it is sent.

Who wrote what

  • Every answer says who answered: Muse Spark, Muse Spark Open, Muse Glimmer or the fixed answers, and what it looked up.
  • The plan says who wrote it, and a filming tip says which model chose it when the model's choice was used.

What leaves this server

  • An upload's measurements reach a hosted model only when its player agreed to that route at upload, and confirmed they are 18 or older.
  • The models that write get your questions, the decided remarks, the take's measured summary and what the lookups return, never your video or its sound. Only the filming tip sends 4 still frames, at most 768 pixels on their long side, and only when its box was ticked.
  • SAM 3.1 gets the frames wherever it runs, as its line above says: this server, our own copy on a rented GPU, or Meta's hosted API.

What each input judges, and what it rests on

A phone video gives its sound and a view of your hands 5 times a second; your piano's MIDI gives the exact notes; the demos, recorded in the Museverse Quest app, give the piano's MIDI and the headset's hand tracking. Each item is judged only from an input that can measure it.

Tested on a professional's own playing

A professional pianist's own takes go through the same code a learner's do, and must give no remark about technique and no wrong, missed or added note; praise only where it is said inside what is measured. A remark that survives is left out for that input, or its measure is fixed; a limit is never raised just to silence it. The recordings are kept by the team and are not part of this code, so a copy without them skips this test.

What it cannot do

  • Piano only. The transcription model and the keyboard model are both made for a piano keyboard.
  • Quiet notes. Half of the notes played below velocity 30 are missed, so a soft passage is judged on fewer notes than were played.
  • Framing. To place your hands it needs the keyboard in the picture and either one end of it, or 20 notes played with both hands fully in view to tell the octave from; otherwise it judges from the notes alone (the sound, or your piano's MIDI) and says so.
  • Not judged on a phone video. Which finger played each note, wrist angles and finger curvature (from one camera the error is larger than the effect), how far a hand stretches and how it arrives after a jump (the picture is read 5 times a second, and shows a hand's width only roughly), tension or pain, and pedalling. The demos, recorded in the Museverse Quest app, are judged from the headset's hand tracking instead, which adds, when it tracks the hands 60 times a second or more, a wrist held low or high, a hand rolled towards the little finger and a little finger lifted (each only against the player's own playing in that take, and never praised), and the arrival after a jump.
  • Not judged from the sound. Loudness, which it hears only roughly; how evenly a fast run is spaced, since a misheard or missed note changes the spacing; how long notes are held, and the pedal.
  • Tried live only once. One question was answered live, by Muse Spark through OpenRouter, on Oct 26, 2026. Every other model route on this page (Meta's Model API, SAM 3.1 hosted at Meta or on a rented GPU, the open-weights hosts, a self-hosted Muse Glimmer and Muse Voice Transcribe) has run only against stand-ins on our machine so far, nothing has run on a GPU yet, and no phone video filmed by our own players has been analysed yet, as of Oct 28, 2026.
  • No learner study. Its limits have not been checked against a teacher's judgement yet. The professional null tests above show it stays quiet on a professional's own playing, not that it finds a learner's faults: nobody has measured whether a teacher agrees with its findings, or whether players who follow them improve.
